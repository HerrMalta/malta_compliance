# Capital allowances in Malta

Legal basis of the tax deductions for capital expenditure of a self-employed individual in Malta: wear and tear of plant and machinery, industrial buildings and structures, and capital expenditure on intellectual property. Each statement cites the provision it rests on. Statements resting on official guidance without legal force name that guidance. Points that no source states are listed in [Readings](#readings) with the reading applied and its grounds.

## Terms

- **Year of assessment** and **basis year**: the deductions of a year of assessment are those of the year immediately preceding it (ITA art. 14(1), opening words). An individual makes up the accounts of a trade or business to 31 December (art. 11(1)). The Commissioner's permission for another accounting date is open only to companies, commercial partnerships, bodies corporate established by law and undertakings under art. 30(7)(d) (art. 11(2) and (3)).
- **Plant and machinery**: the Act does not define the term. S.L. 123.01 classifies it into the 18 categories of its Schedule, which include computer software and furniture.
- **Industrial building or structure**: defined in ITA art. 2 as including a building used as a hotel, a car park or offices, as prescribed; art. 2 also defines "hotel" and "car park". The core term is not defined further.
- **Capital expenditure**: not deductible save as provided in art. 14 and 23 (art. 26(c)); the cost of improvements is not deductible (art. 26(d)).

## Plant and machinery: wear and tear

### Deduction

- ITA art. 14(1)(f) allows a deduction for the wear and tear of plant and machinery "arising out of the use or employment of such property in the production of the income".
- The deduction is computed on the straight-line method over the minimum number of years prescribed in the Schedule to S.L. 123.01 (rule 3). The IRD guide accepts a number of years above the minimum (TRA 05).
- An item that fits more than one category is classified by its preponderating use in each year (rule 4).
- The deduction is allowed only where proper records and documentation of the cost are kept (rule 5). Expenditure on a supply for which the supplier has to issue a tax invoice under the VAT Act is deductible only if the person holds that invoice (ITA art. 14(5)).
- The cost of an asset is determined in accordance with generally accepted accounting principles (rule 6).
- The aggregate of the deductions under art. 14(1)(f) and (j) does not exceed the original cost, having regard to the extent to which the asset was used wholly and exclusively in the production of the income (art. 14(1)(f), proviso (iii)).
- Plant and machinery with deductions allowed before year of assessment 2002 continues on its written-down value, which is deemed to be its cost (rule 12).

### Start of the allowance and first year

- The deduction arises from the use or employment of the asset in the production of the income (art. 14(1)(f)). An asset acquired but not yet used in the business receives no allowance until the year in which it is first used.
- An asset acquired or completed on any date other than the first day of the year receives the deduction as if it had been used for the whole year (S.L. 123.01 rule 7(a)). The first year therefore receives a full year's allowance, whatever the date of acquisition.
- Expenditure incurred before the business starts is treated as incurred on the first day of business only for the categories prescribed under art. 14(3): staff training, salaries or wages, and advertising (S.L. 123.62). These are revenue items; capital items receive allowances from the year of first use under the rules above.

### Year of disposal

- An asset sold or otherwise transferred, destroyed, or put out of use as worn out, obsolete, useless or no longer required, on any date other than the last day of the year, receives no deduction for the year of that event (S.L. 123.01 rule 7(b)).
- An event on the last day of the year leaves the full deduction of that year in place.

### Initial deduction

- ITA art. 14(1)(j) still contains an initial deduction of one fifth of the capital expenditure on plant and machinery first used in the basis year. Its second proviso, added by Act IV of 2001 (art. 35 and Schedule), ends this deduction from a year of assessment fixed by ministerial order.
- The order is L.N. 12 of 2002: under the second proviso to art. 14(1)(j), the provisions of that article "shall be deemed to have come into force as from year of assessment 2002". Plant and machinery therefore receives no initial deduction from year of assessment 2002 (basis year 2001).
- The IRD guide confirms it: the workbook part for plant and machinery acquired from year of assessment 2002 holds no initial allowance, and the initial allowance exists only in the part for industrial buildings and structures (TRA 05).
- The initial deduction of one tenth for industrial buildings and structures is not affected.

### Private use

- An asset used both in the production of the income and for other purposes receives the deduction computed under the rules, reduced in the proportion of its use in the production of the income to its total use, in each year (S.L. 123.01 rule 11).
- The same proportion reduces a balancing allowance or charge (ITA art. 24(2), proviso (ii)).
- Private expenses are not deductible (art. 26(a) and (b)).

### Assets not owned by the user

- Where the burden of wear and tear falls on the user of an asset that does not belong to the user, the user receives the deduction as if the asset belonged to the user (ITA art. 14(1)(f); S.L. 123.01 rule 10). This covers hire purchase, which is not a finance lease (S.L. 123.88 rule 3(2)).
- Under a qualifying finance lease the lessee deducts the full lease payments and receives no wear and tear deduction; the lessor receives the wear and tear deduction (S.L. 123.88 rule 5). The deduction of the lease payments applies "notwithstanding the provisions of rule 3" of S.L. 123.07, so the vehicle restriction does not limit it.
- A qualifying finance lease requires a licensed finance leasing company as lessor, a term of at least four years and not shorter than the period in the Schedule to S.L. 123.01, and notification to the Commissioner within three months (rule 4). Hire purchase, lease purchase, operating leases and sale and leaseback are not finance leases (rule 3(2)). "Asset" excludes industrial buildings and structures (rule 2). Early termination is governed by rule 7.
- A payment of the lessee on transfer of ownership above the agreed annual payments receives wear and tear deductions, and art. 24 applies to it (S.L. 123.88 rule 6).

### Transfers between related persons and without consideration

- An asset acquired from a person controlling, or controlled by, the acquirer to more than 50%, or where both are controlled to more than 50% by the same persons, receives deductions computed on the lower of the transferor's written-down value (after any balancing allowance or charge) and the acquirer's cost (S.L. 123.01 rule 8).
- An asset acquired other than by onerous title receives no greater annual deduction than the previous owner would have received (rule 9).

### Motor vehicles: cost cap

- A vehicle acquired at a cost above EUR 14,000 and used in the production of the income has its wear and tear deduction, its initial deduction and any balancing allowance or charge under art. 24 computed as if the cost were EUR 14,000 (S.L. 123.07 rule 3(1)).
- The amount was Lm 3,000 (L.N. 322 of 2001, in force from 1 January 2001), EUR 7,000 from 1 January 2008 (L.N. 409 of 2007, Schedule II item 34) and is EUR 14,000 from year of assessment 2011 (L.N. 369 of 2009, rule 1(2): "These rules shall be applicable as from the year of assessment 2011").
- The amount applies by the year of assessment of the claim, also to a vehicle acquired under an earlier amount. Rule 3(1) covers a vehicle acquired "in a year immediately preceding any year of assessment" and names no period of acquisition; L.N. 369 of 2009 holds no transitional rule and no reference to the date of acquisition, unlike L.N. 322 of 2018 for aircraft ("shall apply for aircraft acquired on or after"); an amended provision continues to have effect "as amended" (Interpretation Act, Cap. 249, art. 12(2)). Example: a car bought in 2009 for EUR 20,000 receives 1,400 for year of assessment 2010 (on 7,000) and 2,800 for year of assessment 2011 (on 14,000). No official statement confirms this reading; see [Readings](#readings).
- A vehicle with allowances on an earlier, lower cap does not reach the later cap within its minimum number of years. The remainder is allowed in the following year while a written-down value remains: the IRD workbook computes each year's amount as the cost divided by the years, limited to the written-down value brought forward, and counts no years (TRA5). Rule 3 of S.L. 123.01 sets a minimum number of years, and art. 14(1)(f) proviso (iii) limits the total to the cost.
- In the balancing statement of a capped vehicle the proceeds are apportioned in the proportion of the capped cost to the actual cost. Rule 3(1) does not state it; the IRD guide does: "the worksheet does not cater for apportionment in the case of motor vehicles the cost of which has been restricted to the statutory Lm3000 limit. You have to apportion proceeds accordingly" (TRA 04).
- The cap applies to any mechanically propelled vehicle constructed or adapted as a means of transport for individuals. It does not apply to a vehicle of a type not commonly used as a private vehicle and unsuitable to be so used, to a vehicle used wholly or mainly for hire to or the carriage of members of the public in the ordinary course of the trade or business, or, from year of assessment 2002, to a vehicle used wholly or mainly for driving instruction (rule 3(4)).
- For a leased vehicle with a listed price above EUR 14,000, the deductible lease payments are limited to the lease payments multiplied by 14,000 and divided by the listed price; where the burden of wear and tear is on the lessee, no lease payment is deductible (rule 3(2) and (3)).
  - "Lease payments" include insurance, licences, repairs and servicing borne by the lessee and exclude fuel and garaging (rule 3(3)(c)).
  - A vehicle is deemed leased under any arrangement with a payment for its use (rule 3(3)(b)).
  - The restriction does not apply to occasional use of less than seven days (rule 3(2)(b)); a temporary replacement for less than thirty days without a change of the payments keeps the listed price of the replaced vehicle (rule 3(2)(c)).
- The deduction of 150% or 125% of the cost of new electric or hybrid vehicles under S.L. 123.124 applies to companies only ("qualifying person" means a company carrying on a trade or business, rule 2).

### Low-value items and renewals

- Capital expenditure is not deductible save as provided in art. 14 and 23 (art. 26(c)). No provision of the Act, S.L. 123.01 or S.L. 123.07 exempts capital items below an amount from the straight-line rules.
- Expenditure on the renewal, repair or alteration of an implement, utensil or article employed in acquiring the income is deductible under ITA art. 14(1)(c).

### Unabsorbed allowances

- Deductions under art. 14(1)(f) and (j) that exceed the profits of the source of income in respect of which they are allowable are added to the deduction for wear and tear of that source in the following year, and so on for subsequent years (art. 14(1)(f), proviso (ii); art. 14(1)(j), first proviso).
- A trading loss under art. 14(1)(g) is computed without the deductions under paragraphs (f) and (j). Capital allowances therefore do not create a loss set off against other income.
- The MTCA guidance note on car parks states the same principle for industrial buildings: the deduction is allowable only against the income generated by the operation of the car park.

## Disposals: balancing statement (ITA art. 24)

### Events and statement

- Art. 24 applies to plant and machinery and to industrial buildings and structures owned and employed by a person in the production of the income, for which deductions under art. 14(1)(f) and (j) were allowed (art. 24(1) and (7)(a)).
- It applies where the asset or a part of it is sold or otherwise transferred under an onerous title, destroyed, or put out of use as worn out, obsolete, useless or no longer required, before the source of income has ceased (art. 24(1)). An event after the source has ceased produces no balancing statement.
- An asset acquired and disposed of in the same year, on a date other than the last day, receives no deduction (rule 7(b)); see [Readings](#readings) for the balancing statement.
- An asset taken into private use is put out of use as no longer required (art. 24(1)(c)). Item (iii) is then its disposal value, the open market value at that time (art. 24(7)(d)), not zero.
- The person renders a balancing statement with the return of income for the year of assessment (art. 24(1)). It shows:
  - (i) the capital expenditure on the provision of the asset;
  - (ii) the total depreciation by wear and tear since acquisition, taking into account all deductions allowed under art. 14(1)(f) and (j) and earlier balancing allowances or charges;
  - (iii) the sale, insurance, salvage or compensation monies, the value received in an exchange, or the disposal value of an asset put out of use.
- Capital expenditure, sale price and disposal value are the open market values in the opinion of the Commissioner (art. 24(7)(b) to (d)).
- An asset transferred to an employee as a fringe benefit is treated in the balancing statement as put out of use for no value, or as sold for the consideration paid; no deduction is allowed for the benefit itself (S.L. 123.07 rule 4(3)(a)).

### Balancing allowance and balancing charge (art. 24(2))

- Balancing allowance (deduction) = (i) - (ii) - (iii), where (i) exceeds (ii) + (iii).
- Balancing charge (addition to income) = (ii) + (iii) - (i), where (ii) + (iii) exceeds (i). A balancing charge is deemed to be income chargeable with tax (art. 4(5)).
- The balancing charge does not exceed the aggregate deductions allowed under art. 14(1)(f) and (j) and included in item (ii) (proviso (i)). The IRD guide applies this as "proceeds are automatically restricted where these exceed cost" (TRA 04).
- For an asset used partly in the production of the income, the allowance or charge is reduced having regard to the extent of that use (proviso (ii)).
- A balancing allowance that exceeds the profits of the source is carried forward to the following years while the source continues to exist (art. 24(8)).
- Immovable property taxed on transfer under art. 5A: where deductions under art. 14(1)(f) and (j) were allowed, the balancing statement is still rendered, and a balancing charge is taxed at the rates of art. 56 (art. 5A(12B)).

For an asset without private use, without the vehicle cap and sold for no more than its cost, item (i) - (ii) equals the written-down value, and the balancing allowance or charge equals the difference between the proceeds and the written-down value.

### Replacement of the asset (art. 24(3))

Where the asset is replaced and a balancing charge arises, the owner may elect by notice in writing to the Commissioner:

- if the charge exceeds the capital expenditure on the new asset: the charge is reduced to the difference, and the new asset receives no deductions under art. 14(1)(f) and (j); for a later balancing statement of the new asset, its deductions are deemed equal to its full cost (art. 24(3)(a));
- otherwise: no charge is made, the deductions on the new asset are computed on its capital expenditure reduced by the charge that would have been made, and for a later balancing statement its deductions are deemed increased by that charge (art. 24(3)(b)).

For a new asset used only partly in the production of the income, only the corresponding part of its capital expenditure is taken into account.

### Commissioner's determination

The Commissioner may accept the statement or determine the allowance or charge to the best of his judgment (art. 24(4)), and may assess a balancing charge where no statement was delivered (art. 24(5)). Both determinations are open to appeal (art. 24(6)).

## Industrial buildings and structures

- The deduction under art. 14(1)(f) for premises being an industrial building or structure does not exceed 2% a year of the cost, excluding the cost of the land on which the building or structure is erected (proviso (i) and proviso (iii)(b)).
- An initial deduction of one tenth of the capital expenditure applies to premises being an industrial building or structure first used and employed in the basis year (art. 14(1)(j)). It applies to a new building only: the IRD workbook enters the additions of the year as "New" or "Used" and computes the initial deduction as 10% of the additions entered as new, in the year of purchase (TRA5); the IRD guide: "The initial allowance is only calculated on assets indicated as new" (TRA 05). A building used before by another person receives the 2% only. The aggregate of all deductions does not exceed the cost (art. 14(1)(f), proviso (iii)).
- A building purchased in the year receives the full 2% for that year; a building disposed of in the year receives nothing for that year, whatever the date. The IRD workbook holds no factor for the part of a year (TRA5).
- A car park counts as an industrial building or structure if it is of a commercial nature, available to the general public, the main income generating activity of the claimant (or its operation involves substantial activity), and first used as a car park after 1 January 2012 (art. 2; MTCA guidance note on car parks).
- "Offices" means an office business centre first used and occupied on or after 1 January 2016, with more than 2,500 square metres of office space used exclusively for offices, grouped on a single parcel with common services and a common management; only the owner receives the deductions (S.L. 123.173 rule 2).
- The cost of the land is excluded. Where the building is part of a larger building, the land is apportioned by surface area; a real right over the airspace counts as land; unidentified land cost is deemed to be its market value at construction or acquisition (S.L. 123.173 rules 4 to 7).
- A deduction under S.L. 123.26 (immovable property income) or the tax on rental income under art. 31D excludes allowances on the same offices for that year and all later years, and the reverse (S.L. 123.173 rule 8).
- An owner who acquired the property in a transfer exempt under art. 5(9), 5A(4)(f) or 5A(4)(j) is deemed to have acquired it at the lower of the actual consideration and the consideration of the last non-exempt transfer, increased by later construction costs, and on the date of that transfer (S.L. 123.173 rule 3).
- Art. 24 applies to disposals of industrial buildings and structures in the same way as to plant and machinery.
- On the meaning of "industrial building or structure", where no local case law exists, the Administrative Review Tribunal referred to the English law and applied the definitions of the UK Capital Allowances Acts 1990 and 2001 (72/2011, 7 January 2014, confirmed on appeal on 14 October 2016).

## Intellectual property (ITA art. 14(1)(m))

### Spreading over at least three years

- Capital expenditure on intellectual property or intellectual property rights used or employed in the production of the income is deductible (art. 14(1)(m)).
- It is spread equally over at least three consecutive years; the first year is the year in which the expenditure was incurred or the year in which the property was first used or employed in producing the income (first proviso).
- MTCA considers spreading over at least three consecutive years a reasonable manner of spreading the expenditure over the life of the property (MTCA clarification, from year of assessment 2017).

### Deduction in one year (from year of assessment 2024)

- The expenditure may instead be deducted in the year in which it was incurred or in which the property was first used (second proviso, applicable from year of assessment 2024), subject to S.L. 123.07 rule 6.
- The deduction of each year then consists of two parts (S.L. 123.07 rule 6(1)):
  - (a) the part deductible by reference to the minimum period of three years under the first proviso;
  - (b) the remaining expenditure ("accelerated amortisation").
- Part (b) is deductible only against qualifying income: the income produced through the use or employment of the intellectual property, computed before the deductions of rule 6(1)(b) or 6(2) and of art. 14(1)(o), that is after part (a) and before part (b) (S.L. 123.07 rule 2). Part (b) that exceeds the qualifying income is not set off against other income and not surrendered under group relief; it is carried forward to the following years (rule 6(1), first proviso).
- The aggregate of parts (a) and (b) does not exceed the expenditure (rule 6(1), second proviso). Nothing in rule 6 allows a deduction or carry-forward where the amount would not have been an allowable loss under art. 14(1)(g) (rule 6(3)).
- Expenditure brought forward into year of assessment 2024 may also be claimed under the second proviso; part (a) then follows the period chosen in earlier years (rule 6(2)).
- The election is made for each asset separately. Unused accelerated amortisation is attributed to one or more of the assets producing the qualifying income, at the taxpayer's discretion (MTCA guidance note on art. 14(1)(m)).
- Example of the MTCA guidance note: an asset of EUR 3,000,000 first used in year of assessment 2024 receives part (a) of EUR 1,000,000 and part (b) of EUR 2,000,000. The qualifying income of the activity is its chargeable income after the part (a) amortisation of all its assets. Part (b) is used up to that income; EUR 1,700,000 is carried forward. In the following year the amount carried forward is deducted first as part (a) (EUR 1,000,000), and the rest (EUR 700,000) stays part (b); in the final year part (a) is EUR 500,000. The deduction is never slower than the three-year spreading.

### Transfers

- Sums receivable on the transfer of intellectual property held as a capital asset are taxed exclusively under ITA art. 5 (MTCA clarification, point (ii)). Art. 5(1)(a)(ii) lists gains from the transfer of copyright, patents, trademarks, trade names and other intellectual property.
- Art. 24 refers to deductions under art. 14(1)(f) and (j) only. A disposal of intellectual property therefore requires no balancing statement.
- The cost of acquisition in the art. 5 computation excludes the deductions claimed: allowable expenditure excludes "any sum which has been allowed as a deduction for the purposes of Part IV of the Act" (S.L. 123.27 rule 3), and "in the case of a transfer of intellectual property or intellectual property rights, the cost of acquisition shall not include any amount in respect of which a deduction has been claimed in terms of article 14(1)(m) of the Act" (rule 4(2)). Instalments not yet claimed stay in the cost and reduce the gain.
- A capital loss is not set off against other income; it is carried forward against capital gains of later years (ITA art. 5(10)(b)).
- Where the property was transferred by a company without gain or loss under art. 5(9), the acquirer's total deduction is the lower of the cost and the market value at transfer, reduced by the deductions claimed by the transferor (art. 14(1)(m), third proviso).

### Related deductions

- Art. 14(1)(h) spreads capital expenditure on scientific research equally over the year incurred and the five following years, except expenditure on plant, machinery or premises deductible under paragraphs (f) and (j).
- Art. 14(1)(ha), applicable from year of assessment 2027 once commenced by notice, allows 175% of expenditure on research, development and innovation, with the same exclusion of plant, machinery and premises.
- Art. 14(1)(ma) spreads capital expenditure incurred from 1 January 2025 on a business permit, concession or commercial lease over 15 years or the shorter duration of the right. No deduction is allowed for a right of indefinite duration or of more than 15 years (iii), without a written document (iv), or acquired from a related party (ix). Deductions stop on transfer or end of use (vii), and the cost deduction on a later transfer is reduced by the deductions claimed (viii). Paragraph (m) holds no rule corresponding to (viii).

## Computer software

- Computer software is plant and machinery by Schedule item 2 of S.L. 123.01.
- The Copyright Act lists computer programs among literary works (Cap. 415, art. 2); the copyright in software is therefore intellectual property in the sense of ITA art. 5(1)(a)(ii).
- Paragraph (m) holds no exclusion of plant and machinery such as paragraphs (h) and (ha) hold.

| Route | Provision | Period | Disposal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wear and tear | S.L. 123.01, Schedule item 2 | Straight line over 4 years, full first year (rule 7(a)), none in the year of disposal (rule 7(b)) | Balancing statement, art. 24 |
| Intellectual property | ITA art. 14(1)(m), first proviso | Equal spreading over at least 3 consecutive years | Art. 5; no balancing statement |
| Intellectual property, one year | ITA art. 14(1)(m), second proviso; S.L. 123.07 rule 6 | Year incurred or first used; part above one third only against income produced by the software | Art. 5; no balancing statement |

Whether a non-exclusive licence to use software acquired from a third party is an intellectual property right within art. 14(1)(m) is listed under [Readings](#readings).

## Improvements to business premises

- Electrical and plumbing installations and sanitary fittings (item 9, 15 years), fixtures and fittings (item 4, 10 years), lifts and escalators (item 14, 10 years) and air-conditioners (item 15, 6 years) are plant and machinery under S.L. 123.01, also when installed in rented premises where the burden of wear and tear falls on the tenant (art. 14(1)(f); rule 10).
- Structural works on a building that is not an industrial building or structure receive no deduction: capital expenditure is excluded save as provided in art. 14 and 23 (art. 26(c)), and the cost of improvements is excluded (art. 26(d)). Repairs remain deductible under art. 14(1)(c).

## Return of income

- The individual return asks for the net profit of each source of trade, business, profession or vocation. A profit and loss account signed by the taxpayer or the accountant is attached; MTCA provides a sample (Tax return guide, year of assessment 2025, section 2).
- The sample profit and loss account has the lines 22 "Amortisation of intangible assets", 23 "Depreciation of Immovable Property" and 24 "Depreciation of Tangible Assets", and no section for tax adjustments.
- The balancing statement is rendered with the return (ITA art. 24(1)). The download pages of mtca.gov.mt for individuals and self-employed persons hold no form for a capital allowance schedule or a balancing statement.
