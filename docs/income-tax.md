# Income tax, provisional tax and Class 2 contributions in Malta

Legal basis of three computations for a resident self-employed individual in Malta, single or married: the income tax of a basis year, the provisional tax paid during the year, and the Class 2 social security contributions of a self-occupied person. Each statement cites its provision. Statements resting on official guidance without legal force name that guidance. Points stated by no source are listed in [Readings](#readings) with the reading applied and its grounds.

## Terms

- **Basis year** and **year of assessment**: the tax of a year of assessment is charged on the income of the year immediately preceding it, the basis year (ITA art. 4(1) and 14(1)). An individual makes up the accounts of a trade to 31 December (art. 11(1)).
- **Return boxes**: the numbers of the individual return of income as described in the MTCA return guide. Box 2 is the net profit of each trade, box 8 the total emolument and business income of each spouse, box 15 the other income, box 22 the deductions, box 25 the chargeable income, box 26 the tax, box 27 the tax charged, box 36 the provisional tax paid and box 42 the tax underpaid or overpaid.
- **Self-occupied person**: a person engaged in an activity through which earnings exceeding EUR 910 a year are derived (SSA art. 2). A self-occupied person pays Class 2 contributions under Part III of the Tenth Schedule. A "self-employed person" in the sense of the SSA is a person with other income only (Part II of the Schedule) and is outside this note.
- **Contribution year**: the period from the first Monday in January to the last Sunday before the first Monday in January of the following year (SSA art. 2). A contribution week is counted by its Monday.

## Income tax

### Chargeable income

- The income of a trade is the profit after all outgoings and expenses wholly and exclusively incurred in the production of the income (ITA art. 14(1)). Domestic or private expenses are not deductible unless the Act allows them specifically (art. 26(a)).
- Capital allowances are part of the profit of the source (art. 14(1)(f), (j) and (m); [capital-allowances.md](capital-allowances.md)).
- A trading loss is set off against other income and capital gains of the year and carried forward against the income of later years (art. 14(1)(g)). The return takes a loss of the year in box 2 and losses brought forward in box 21, a part of the deductions of box 22; the record takes them as a deduction.
- The return holds the net profit of each trade in box 2, from a profit and loss account attached to it. Neither the return nor the MTCA sample profit and loss account has a line for the contributions of the taxpayer.
- Interest is investment income of return boxes 9 and 10, or subject to a final withholding tax, and not part of the trade profit. The app leaves the interest income on account 4920 of its chart out of the profit and names it in the report; sundry income on account 4910 stays in the profit.
- Class 2 contributions of the taxpayer are not deducted; see [Readings](#readings). The contributions paid by an employer for employees are expenses of the trade.
- Personal deductions (fees for schools, childcare, sports and homes for the elderly, alimony) and tax credits are claimed with forms and certificates in boxes 16 to 20 and 28 to 31 (return guide). The app takes them as entered amounts.

### Married couples

- The income of a married couple living together is charged in the name of the responsible spouse, and both spouses sign one return (ITA art. 49).
- The responsible spouse may elect a separate computation: the tax on the income of the other spouse from a trade, business, profession or vocation, from employment, or from a pension for past employment is computed separately. All other income of the spouses is aggregated with the income of the spouse with the higher income of those kinds, or with the income of the responsible spouse where both are equal (art. 50(1)).
- The joint computation uses the rates of art. 56(1)(a), the separate computation the rates of art. 56(1)(b) for each spouse.
- The return computes both and takes the lower tax in box 27: "the LOWER of: the total of boxes 26j and 26k, OR the amount in box 26l" (return form, box 27). The return guide lets a couple apply the separate computation where it is "more advantageous to them".
- From year of assessment 2021 spouses may elect separate returns under the conditions of art. 49A. The Budget Speech 2027 announces a project under which each spouse files an individual return as a general rule, with a joint declaration by selection; it names no date.

Conditions of the categories:

- **Parent**: the individual maintained a child under his or her custody, or paid maintenance for it, and the child was not over 18 years of age, or not over 23 years in full-time education (art. 56(1)(b)(ii)). The Act as consolidated up to 2024 and the return guide for basis year 2024 also require a child without gainful occupation or with earnings up to EUR 3,400; the app does not check it.
- **Tables for taxpayers with children**: a child as above, and the individual or a spouse is a national of an EU or EEA state or a long-term resident; where neither spouse is such a national, the child was born in Malta and is resident there (art. 56(1)(a)(ii), (iii) and (b)(iv), (v)).
- **Single parent**: an unmarried, widowed, separated or divorced individual wholly maintaining a child under sole custody is charged under the married table (a)(i), unless a table of (b)(iv) or (v) is more beneficial (art. 56(1)(b)(iii)).
- Act III of 2026 prints 10,270 as the amount subtracted above 60,000 in the table "Parent, one child", in the English and the Maltese text, as does the Budget Speech 2026. The tax then rises by 5 at 60,000. MTCA's table of 2026 prints 10,275. The register holds the amount of the Act.

### Rounding

The return guide rounds the boxes as follows: in determining the chargeable income any fraction of a euro is disregarded; a deduction against income is rounded up to one euro; in computing the tax due, a fraction of a euro equal to or less than fifty cents is disregarded and a higher fraction is rounded up to one euro.

### Rebate for certain income brackets

The tables of Act XV of 2016 raise the tax at one band limit each: by 90 at 19,500 (single), by 105 at 21,200 (parent) and by 120 at 28,700 (married). Section 26A of the return guide removes the step: a taxpayer with a chargeable income above the limit and below 19,620, 21,340 or 28,860 receives a rebate of 17,440, 19,055 or 25,550 less the chargeable income after tax. No version of the Act holds this rebate; see [Readings](#readings). The tables from basis year 2025 have no such step, and the return guide for basis year 2025 holds no such section.

### Payment, interest and late return

- The return is due on 30 June of the year of assessment, and the tax becomes due and payable on the same date, the tax settlement date (S.L. 372.16 rules 2(c)(ii) and 5(b); ITMA art. 42(1A) and (3)(c)).
- The tax to pay is the tax charged less tax credits, tax deducted at source and the provisional tax paid for the basis year (return boxes 27 to 42).
- Tax unpaid after the settlement date bears interest for each month or part of a month: 0.33% for periods from 1 January 2020, and 0.6% where the tax is payable on 31 August 2022 or later; the total interest does not exceed the tax (ITMA art. 44(2A); S.L. 372.28 rule 2).
- An individual in default with the return is charged additional tax by the months since the return date: 10 within 6 months, 50 within 12, 100 within 18, 150 within 24, 200 within 36, 300 within 48, 400 within 60 and 500 later (ITA art. 56(12)(c) and Schedule, item 2). The app does not compute this amount.

## Provisional tax

- **Liability**: provisional tax is paid in the basis year on account of the tax of the following year of assessment (ITMA art. 42(2); S.L. 372.18 rule 3(1)). A provisional tax payer is an individual who was liable to tax in Malta for the benchmark year of assessment, other than one eligible for the election of ITMA art. 12 (rule 2).
- **Benchmark year of assessment**: the last year of assessment in respect of which a return was due before the start of the calendar year in which the first payment falls due (rule 2). For a basis year N this is year of assessment N - 1, the basis year N - 2.
- **Benchmark**: the tax payable for the benchmark year of assessment as shown in the self-assessment, adding back the provisional tax taken into account in it (rule 6(1)). The booklet gives the example of a tax of 1,200 with 200 deducted at source: the benchmark is 1,000.
- **Other benchmarks**: an assessment or a determination of the Commissioner replaces the self-assessment (rules 6(3) and 7(1)(a)); the Commissioner may fix a benchmark by notice (rule 6(4)). Where the self-assessment of the benchmark year was not furnished, the last earlier self-assessment furnished is used (rule 7(1)(b)); without any, the benchmark is nil (rule 7(1)(c)). The record takes such a benchmark as an entered amount.
- **Instalments**: at least 20% of the benchmark by 30 April, a further 30% by 31 August and a further 50% by 21 December of the basis year (rules 4(1) and 5), each amount rounded up or down to the nearest euro (rule 3(3)).
- **First years**: "If 2019 is your first year of business you are not obliged to pay any PT"; for a first year 2018 the benchmark for 2020 is based on the tax payable for 2018 (booklet, question 11). No provisional tax is therefore due in the first and the second year.
- **Reduction**: a payer expecting a lower tax may limit the payments to the estimated current year liability by the P.T. Reduction Form. A payment falling due after the form is the lower of its amount under rule 5 and the excess of the estimate over the provisional tax already paid; the payments due before it keep their amount (rule 10(3)). The record holds the estimate and the date of the form.
- **Additional tax**: provisional tax not paid when due bears additional tax of 1% for each calendar month or part of a month; 0.33% for periods from 1 January 2020 and 0.6% for periods from 1 June 2022 (rule 14(1)). No month starting after the tax settlement date is charged (rule 14(2)). Where the tax of the self-assessment is less than the benchmark, the additional tax on the difference is reduced by 90% (rule 14(1), last proviso).
- **Appropriation and credit**: a payment discharges the provisional tax of the period that has fallen due, and an excess goes to the amounts not yet due (rule 13(1)). Provisional tax is set off against the tax of the year of assessment; an excess is refunded (rule 13(2)). A record lists the payments made in its calendar year; a payment made after the end of the year is listed on the record of the following year with a kind naming the preceding year.

## Class 2 contributions

- **Liability**: every self-occupied person pays a contribution (SSA art. 10(1)); the State adds half of it (art. 10(4)). A self-occupied person is a self-employed person, and a self-employed person is not an employed person (art. 2): a person in insurable employment pays Class 1 contributions and no Class 2 contributions on a side trade. The record holds a switch for such a person.
- **Base**: the annual net earnings during the calendar year immediately preceding the contribution year in which the contribution is paid (Tenth Schedule Part III). Earnings are the income from the economic activity net of the expenses directly incurred in generating it, without interest, rents and other income not related to the activity, and without income of the spouse (art. 2). The app takes the net profit of the trades of the person, without the interest income on account 4920.
- **Categories**: SA is a fixed weekly rate for earnings up to the first limit. SB is the weekly equivalent of 15% of the annual net earnings, calculated to the nearest cent. SC is a fixed weekly rate for earnings above the second limit. The second limit and the SC rate are higher for persons born on or after 1 January 1962 (Tenth Schedule Part III, notes).
- **Weekly equivalent**: 15% of the net earnings divided by 52 (booklet, question 20), calculated to the nearest cent (Schedule). The booklet's own example drops the third decimal (1,500 divided by 52 given as 28.84); MTCA's calculator returns 43.27 for a net profit of 15,000 in 2025, the nearest cent, with 17, 17 and 18 weeks.
- **First year**: in the first calendar year of the business a self-occupied person pays the lowest rate SA, because no net income of a preceding year exists (Social Security Department).
- **Weeks and due dates**: the contributions are paid in arrears for the four months ending on the last day of April, August and December (art. 11). MTCA demands them with the provisional tax, by 30 April, 31 August and 21 December, for the number of Mondays of January to April, May to August and September to December (booklet, question 21).
- **Late payment**: a further contribution of 1% a month of the unpaid contributions is due for periods after 2 January 2000 (art. 116(1)(c)). From 1 January 2026 it is replaced by the interest of ITMA art. 44(2A) and S.L. 372.28 (art. 116(1), proviso added by Act III of 2026 art. 27): 0.6% a month for an amount payable on 31 August 2022 or later, 0.33% for an earlier one, in total not above the amount.
- **Arrears**: until Act III of 2026, contributions payable under art. 116 by a person born on or after 1 January 1962 were "payable at the rate at which the contribution will be payable at the time of payment" (art. 116(5), first proviso, version of 2021-07-16). Act III of 2026 art. 27(b) replaced the proviso by the interest above. The app does not apply the earlier proviso; see [Not covered](#not-covered).
- **Wrong rate**: a difference between the rate paid and the rate due is payable or refundable under art. 116(1)(d) and (5); a request for a refund lapses after five years.
- **Income tax**: the Income Tax Act names no deduction for these contributions; see [Readings](#readings).

| Months of additional tax | Counted from the calendar month after the month of the due date, up to the month of payment | Rule 14(1) charges "each calendar month or part thereof"; the booklet counts 6 months, January to June, for an instalment due on 21 December and unpaid until the settlement date |
| Reduction of the additional tax where the final tax is below the benchmark | The final tax, as filed where the return is filed, covers the instalments in the order they fall due and, within an instalment, the parts paid first; 90% of the additional tax on the provisional tax above it is remitted, also where that part was paid late | Rule 14(1), last proviso ("the additional tax chargeable on this difference"); booklet, question 7: with instalments of 400, 600 and 1,000 and a final tax of 1,000, the additional tax on the third instalment is the difference reduced; rule 13(1) applies a payment first to the tax that has fallen due |
| Provisional tax "already paid" under a P.T. Reduction Form | The provisional tax paid up to the due date of the preceding instalment | Rule 10(3)(b) names "the total provisional tax already paid" without a date; a payment made after the preceding due date is the payment of the instalment itself |
| Net earnings of the preceding year for Class 2 | The net profit of the trades as declared in the return, after capital allowances and before the contributions; the earnings of a part-year are not annualised | The Schedule names the "annual net earnings during the calendar year immediately preceding"; MTCA's payment form and contribution calculator ask for the "Net Profit" of the preceding year and for no period of activity; the MTCA profit and loss account ends with the net profit after the depreciation lines |
| Net earnings at or below the SA limit, including a loss | Category SA | The Schedule charges SA where the earnings are "less than" the limit and names no lower limit; MTCA's table prints 910 as the lower limit because a person below it is not self-occupied |
| Due date of Class 2 contributions | 30 April, 31 August and 21 December | SSA art. 11 names the last day of April, August and December; MTCA demands the contributions with the provisional tax (booklet, question 21) |
| Months of the Class 2 addition | Counted as for provisional tax; the months up to December 2025 at 1%, the months from January 2026 at the interest rate of S.L. 372.28 for the date the contribution was payable | The Social Security Department describes the further contribution as running "from the time of due payment to the time of effective payment"; no source gives an example, and none states how the interest applies to contributions due before 2026 |
| Class 2 tables of 2025 to 2027 | Applied from MTCA's tables and marked as such | No Legal Notice substitutes the Tenth Schedule for these years yet |

## Not covered

- Other regimes of the Income Tax Act: the final tax on rental income (art. 31D), the tax on part-time work (art. 90A), investment income subject to a final withholding tax, the pension exemption and rebate, overseas employment, non-residents and the minimum tax of non-domiciled individuals. Income under these regimes is left out of the record.
- Directors' fees of a spouse, which stay with the responsible spouse in a separate computation (ITA art. 50(1)), and the relief of double taxation (return box 33).
- The amounts of personal deductions and tax credits; they are entered as claimed.
- The limits of the tax computation named in [capital-allowances.md](capital-allowances.md): unabsorbed allowances and part (b) of the one-year option for intellectual property.
- Class 2 contributions of full-time farmers, the pro-rata option of part-time self-occupied women, students and pensioners, persons with other income only (category SP), and the end of the liability at the age of 65.
- Arrears of Class 2 contributions of the years up to 2025 paid in a later calendar year by a person born from 1962: the rate of the time of payment under the former art. 116(5) proviso. The report shows the rate of the contribution year with the monthly addition and names the proviso.
- A change of the tax status during a year: marriage, separation or death.
- The additional tax for a late return and the interest on tax unpaid after the settlement date; the report names the rate.
